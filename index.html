How to use the twin

1
Pick a plant. The tabs at the top open the five testbeds. Each one replays a real period of recorded sensor data, the evaluation period on which the detectors were tested.
2
Choose a detector. In the side panel, pick one of the 14 AI detectors and how many labelled incidents it learned from. Its recorded anomaly scores then drive the alarms.
3
Press play (▶ under the plant, or the space bar). Tanks fill, pumps spin and every sensor shows its value and a coloured dot.
4
Watch the alarms. A red badge above the plant means a real incident is in progress; the red frame around the plant blinks while the detector raises an alarm. Ideally the two happen together.
5
Read the live results on the right: how many incidents were caught, how many false alarms, and how quickly.
6
Try the what-if lab (top right) to inject your own faults and watch three detectors running in your browser react.

Words used on this site

Detector
An AI model that gives every minute of data an anomaly score: the higher, the more unusual.
Labelled event
A real incident (an attack, a fault or a quality event) whose start and end are known. Detectors learn from a few of them and are judged on others.
Label budget k
How many labelled events the detector learned from (1 to 14). Zero-label references learn from normal data only.
Label draw
Which random set of k events was used. Every budget was repeated five times.
Alarm threshold θ
The score above which a window raises an alarm. “Validation F1” is tuned on a few labelled examples; “1% budget” allows about one false alarm in a hundred normal windows.
Window
One minute of data; a new window starts every 30 seconds (on GECCO 2018, twelve minutes of data every six minutes).
Deviation z
How far a sensor is from its normal level, in units of its normal variation. The normal level follows the plant (the median of the previous two hours, or the first minutes of a short record), so slow changes do not count.
Event recall
The share of the incidents so far that raised at least one alarm.
False alarm
An alarm episode that touches no incident; also given per hour of normal operation.
Window precision
The share of alarmed windows that really are anomalous.
Event F1
One number that balances event recall and window precision (1 is perfect).
Mean delay
The average time from the start of an incident to its first alarm.
AUROC
How well a detector ranks anomalous windows above normal ones: 1 is perfect, 0.5 is a coin toss.

About this digital twin

What is real. The sensor values are those of the evaluation periods of the five datasets, averaged over each 30-s hop of the 60-s analysis windows (6-min hops of 12-min windows on GECCO 2018). The anomaly scores are the stored per-window scores of the 1,055 benchmark runs: 14 detectors, every label budget k and label draw, and the zero-label references. Thresholds are the run's own: the F1-optimal threshold on the validation split (the default) or the 99th percentile of the validation negatives. Event recall, window precision, event F1, false alarms per hour of normal operation and detection delay are computed live; at the end of the replay they reproduce the recorded results of each run (for 7 of the 1,055 runs, all gradient boosting, a few windows lie within float32 rounding of the threshold, and the replay differs slightly).

What is modelled. The plant drawings are schematic. HAI P1 follows the physical boiler graph shipped with HAI; P2–P4 follow the HAI technical report. SKAB follows the benchmark's testbed description; the valve symbols close during the labelled valve-closing experiments. Smart-Farm-IDS, WDSEventDB and GECCO 2018 are laid out from the dataset descriptions and channel structure. Animations (levels, pump rotation, flows) are driven by the recorded values.

What is simulated. In the what-if lab, injected faults are synthetic changes of the recorded values. The 14 recorded detectors cannot see them (their scores are recorded); three detectors run in your browser instead: point deviation (largest |z|), PCA residual (squared prediction error of the normal correlation structure) and process-graph residual (every point predicted from its neighbours on the process graph). They use the same causal normal reference as the replay, are fitted on the normal record that precedes the evaluation period, and alarm at their 99th percentile there.

Study. B. Boukoftane, J. M. Navarro Ruiz, A. Pita Lozano. Graph Neural Networks versus Tabular Foundation Models for Label-Scarce Anomaly Detection in Water Infrastructure: A Comparison on Five Datasets. The results are preliminary and may change.
